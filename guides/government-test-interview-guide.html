The written test and interview, explained

Every government advertisement ends with two words that decide the outcome: written test and interview. Here is what actually happens at each stage, and how to prepare without buying a course.

Which test will you sit

Recruiting bodyTypical testAfter the test
FPSC (federal)Objective screening test, subject-specific for technical postsViva before the commission
PPSC (Punjab)Written paper at a district centre; separate paper for lecturersInterview at the commission
NTS / PTS / CTSOne MCQ paper set by the testing serviceEmployer's own interview
Departmental / district postsShort written paper or a practical/typing testInterview at the office

The parts that repeat every year

Most screening papers share the same skeleton: English (grammar, comprehension, a short paragraph), general knowledge and Pakistan affairs, basic mathematics, Islamiat or ethics for some posts, and professional questions for the specific job. Preparing the first four properly covers roughly two-thirds of the paper for almost every post.

The five things candidates lose marks on
  • Filling the answer sheet incorrectly — the roll number and the bubbles are checked by machine.
  • Guessing without a strategy; a wrong answer can carry a negative mark in some papers.
  • Running out of time on the maths section instead of banking the easy marks first.
  • Not reading the syllabus printed with the advertisement — it tells you the weighting.
  • Bringing the wrong documents to the centre and being turned away at the gate.

Two weeks of preparation that actually works

  1. Days 1–2: read the advertisement and the syllabus line by line. Write down the exact subjects and their weight. Anything not in the syllabus is not your problem this month.
  2. Days 3–8: one past paper under timed conditions every second day. Between papers, revise only the topics you got wrong — not everything.
  3. Days 9–12: English and Pakistan affairs daily, 40 minutes each. These two carry the most marks across posts.
  4. Days 13–14: two full mock papers, then stop. Sleep and logistics beat one more chapter.

The interview

The viva is short — usually 10 to 20 minutes — and it is not a knowledge test. The panel is checking whether you understand the post you applied for, whether your documents match what you claimed, and how you behave under mild pressure. Expect three kinds of question: about your own application (why this post, what you studied, what you have done), about the department and its work, and one situational question about a problem you might actually face on the job.

Answer in complete sentences, admit what you do not know, and never argue. If a question is outside your field, say so and offer what you do know. Panels respond better to a calm "I have not worked on that, but I would approach it like this" than to a confident wrong answer.

What to carry

  • The printed roll number slip or the call letter — without it you will not be allowed in.
  • Original CNIC, plus one photocopy.
  • Two or three recent photographs, the same ones used on the form.
  • Original degrees and experience letters, for verification at the interview stage.
  • A black ballpoint, a pencil, a sharpener and a simple calculator only if the advertisement allows it.
