How to spot a fake job advertisement

A real employer never asks a candidate for money. That one rule filters out almost every fake listing — but scammers keep finding new angles, so here is the fuller list.

The eight warning signs

  1. They ask for a fee. Registration, processing, interview, training, seat reservation — a genuine employer charges none of these. Government departments collect only the official challan printed in the advertisement, paid into a bank, never into someone's personal account.
  2. The salary is unrealistic for the work and the experience. “Work from home, earn 200,000 a month, no experience” is a recruitment scheme, not a job.
  3. It arrives from a WhatsApp number or a personal Gmail, not from the company's domain.
  4. No company details. No address, no registration number, no website — or a website registered last month.
  5. The interview is a chat. A real hiring process has a named interviewer and a date.
  6. They want CNIC copies or bank details before any interview.
  7. They push you to decide today. Urgency is a pressure tactic, not a hiring practice.
  8. The advertisement is someone else's photocopy with a phone number pasted over the original contact details.

Common scams in Pakistan

  • The overseas visa scam — a Gulf or Europe job that needs a visa fee paid first.
  • The “government contact” — someone with an “inside link” who will get you appointed for a payment. No such arrangement exists, and the money is gone.
  • The paid leads group — a WhatsApp or Facebook group charging a monthly fee for job leads copied from public sites.
  • The fake HR email — a lookalike domain sending an offer letter and asking for bank details for “salary setup”.

How to check a listing in two minutes

  1. Search the exact job title and employer name. A real vacancy appears in more than one place.
  2. Open the employer's own website and look for a careers page with the same vacancy.
  3. For government posts, check the department or public service commission site and the official portal.
  4. Look at the email domain: a company address is not a free mailbox.
  5. Search the phone number — numbers used in scams usually show up in complaints.

If you have already paid or shared documents

  • Stop all contact and send nothing else.
  • Report the number on the platform where the job appeared.
  • Report it to the FIA's cybercrime wing, which takes complaints online.
  • If you shared bank details, call your bank immediately and block the card or account.
  • If you shared CNIC copies, watch for SIMs or accounts opened in your name.
