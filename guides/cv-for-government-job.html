How to write a CV for a government job

A government shortlisting committee spends about 40 seconds on a CV. It is not looking for creativity — it is checking whether you meet the advertised criteria, in the order the advertisement lists them. This is how to make that check easy.

Use the order the advertisement uses

Private-sector CVs lead with a personal statement. Government CVs lead with the facts. Use this order:

  1. Name exactly as on the CNIC, then father's name, CNIC number, date of birth, domicile district, postal address, phone and email.
  2. Post applied for, with the advertisement number and the closing date. Committees sort applications by post; if the post is not obvious on the first page, the file is set aside.
  3. Academic record in a table: degree, institution, board or university, year, marks and percentage. Start from matric and go upward — the advertisement usually sets a minimum starting point.
  4. Experience with dates, employer, designation and a one-line description of duties. Overlaps and gaps are noticed, so keep the dates exact.
  5. Certificates and computer or language skills, then the document list.

Three mistakes that get a good application rejected

  • A name that does not match the CNIC. Adding "Muhammad" that is not on the card, or spelling a father's name differently on the CV and the form, is the single most common rejection.
  • Claiming experience you cannot prove. Every experience line needs a certificate or a letter that can be verified. Unverifiable experience during the interview stage removes the candidate from the merit list.
  • Sending the wrong number of copies. Advertisements ask for a specific number of copies of each document, attested in a specific way. Read that paragraph twice.
Formatting rules that matter on paper
  • Two pages maximum, single column, no photographs unless asked.
  • Font size 11–12, black text, no colour blocks — many offices still print in black and white.
  • Attach copies in the order the advertisement lists them, and write the post name on the envelope.
  • Keep a photocopy of the complete application. If the original is lost in the post, the copy is your proof.
