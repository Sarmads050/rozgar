BPS pay scales explained — BPS-01 to BPS-22

Every government advertisement says something like “BPS-14”. That number decides your starting pay, your promotion path and often your age limit. Here is what it actually means.

What BPS stands for

BPS is the Basic Pay Scale. The government puts each post on a scale from 01 to 22. The higher the scale, the higher the basic pay and the greater the responsibility. Your salary is not one number: it is basic pay plus allowances (house rent, medical, conveyance and others depending on the post), and those allowances change with the annual budget.

Important: basic pay figures change with every pay revision. Read the pay printed in the advertisement you are applying for.

The groups in practice

ScaleTypical postsUsual qualification
BPS-01 to 04Support staff: naib qasid, chowkidar, sweeper, maliPrimary or middle; sometimes none stated
BPS-05 to 10Clerk, driver, dispatch rider, lab attendant, junior technicianMatric or intermediate
BPS-11 to 16Stenographer, senior clerk, computer operator, sub-engineer, staff nurseIntermediate to diploma or bachelor's
BPS-17The entry officer grade: assistant director, lecturer, section officerBachelor's or master's, usually through a commission test
BPS-18 to 19Deputy director and senior officer rolesMaster's plus years of service
BPS-20 to 22Director general and aboveSeniority and promotion; rarely open to fresh applicants

Why BPS-17 matters

BPS-17 is the line between staff and officer. Posts from BPS-17 upwards are usually filled through a public service commission — FPSC for federal, PPSC for Punjab, SPSC for Sindh, KPPSC for Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, BPSC for Balochistan — or through a departmental test. Expect a written test, sometimes a screening test first, and then an interview.

How to read the pay line

  • “BPS-14” — the post is on that scale. Pay is the scale's basic pay plus allowances.
  • “BPS-14 plus usual allowances” — allowances are added on top, as per government rules.
  • “Contract” or “consolidated” — a fixed monthly amount, not the scale, and often no pension.
  • “Daily wages” — paid per day worked, with no benefits.

Age limit, quota and experience

The advertisement also states the age limit, usually with a general relaxation and separate relaxations for certain categories and regions. Domicile quota decides how many posts are reserved for each province. Experience normally has to come from a recognised employer and be supported by a letter — a CV claim alone is not enough.

The practical tip

If two posts look identical but one is BPS-11 and the other BPS-14, the difference is pay, promotion speed and often the required qualification. Read both advertisements fully before applying.
